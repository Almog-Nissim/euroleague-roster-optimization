# CONTEXT

Vocabulary for this repo. Terms here are resolved: use these names, not synonyms.
Numbers that go stale do not live here. Current values are in
`data/processed/refit_day15_diff.csv`, generated by `src/refit_diff.py` from tracked
result files.

## Cost specification

**`COST_SPEC`** — which cost model builds the prices.
- `market` is production (ADR 0001).
- `club_relative` exists only to reproduce the day-14 numbers. `refit_diff.py` checks
  that it still does, row by row.

Never mix the two in one comparison.

## Money and price

**`cost`** — the price of a player in the optimizer's budget constraint. It is
**dimensionless**.
- **Under `market`:** the player is priced at a league-average club (mean club fixed
  effect), and the price is divided by the season's pool mean, so one unit is "one average
  pool player". The scale for each season is recorded as `cost_scale`.
- **Under `club_relative`:** the price is a ratio to the calibration club's mean salary,
  not normalised.

Do not read a `cost` value as money. The euro axis was tested and rejected (see
`docs/refit-day15-diff.md`).

`roster_optimizer.py` (the single-club demo path) multiplies by `mean_sal` and *is* in euros
of `TARGET_CLUB`. The dashboard does not use that path. Two different units share one name.

**`budget`** — the right-hand side of the LP constraint `Σ cost_i · x_i ≤ B`. Same
dimensionless units as `cost`. The sweep grid runs from `B_LO=8.0` to `B_HI=40.0` in steps
of `0.5`. Under `market`, the slider saturates well below `B_HI`: that is an open product
decision. Not to be confused with `net_eur` (below).

**`net_eur`** — a club's real annual player budget in euros, from `club_budgets_gemini.csv`.
An external input. It does **not** enter the cost model. It is used only for `fit_eur`
and for the Day 9 diagnostic of how β₁ varies with club wealth.

**`gross_eur`** — deprecated. Empty for every 2025 row. Still silently filters rows in
`score_to_wins.py` via an inner join. Do not use; remove the dependency.

**`salary_mid`** — a real observed player salary in euros, from `salary_anchors.csv`
(verified player codes) or `salary_external_2025.csv` (name-matched). This is ground truth.
It is the only "salary" in the repo that means what it says.

**`fit_eur`** — an affine display map from `budget` to `net_eur`, fit on club points.
**Club level by construction.** Showing a euro figure next to a single player is
outside what the fit was built for. Whether to show one at all is an open product
decision: one player currently displays a negative amount.

## Calibration and evaluation splits

**`usage`** — the column in both salary files that controls how a row may be used.

| value | `salary_anchors.csv` | `salary_external_2025.csv` | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `calibrate` | 39 rows · TEL | 38 rows · 13 clubs | the market cost model is fit on these |
| `test` | 19 rows · HTA | 20 rows · BAS, HTA, OLY | held out; never touched by a fit |
| `structure_only` | 51 rows · HTA, OLY, PAN | 8 rows · TEL, OLY, PAN | squad structure, not pricing |

Splits are **by club, not by row**: a club goes entirely to one side, because the market
specification uses club fixed effects. Any new salary source must be assigned a `usage`
value before it touches a model.

## Advantage metrics

**`adv_free`** — engine roster quality against the actual club, with no usage
constraint. Median over the 38 club-seasons.

**`adv_cap`** — the same, under the ball-identity usage constraint. `adv_cap` is the
headline metric: it is what the dashboard shows and what the project defends.
`headline_capped_wins` is `adv_cap` converted to wins.

**Do not** compare the engine's `q` against a club's *predicted* `q`. That inflates the
result by roughly 12 percentage points and produces a spurious ~30% figure. Use
`usage_constrained_results.csv`. This is recorded in `meta.fair_compare_warning`.

**curse cost** — `adv_ideal − adv`: the advantage lost by optimising on predicted `ppm`
instead of true `ppm`, with both rosters scored on true production. It is stored as a
share; ×100 gives percentage points, not percent. It is not selection bias. Selection bias
is `bias_*`, and it measures near zero.

## Minutes

**minute cap** (`MAX_MIN_PLAYER = 32`) — the per-player ceiling on expected season minutes
in the LP. It is the **maximum observed season-average minutes** (Day 9,
`minute_profile.py`), not a rule of the game and not a model of fatigue.

Fatigue tiers were designed to replace it and were not adopted (ADR 0004). The box-score
estimate was dominated by how coaches allocate minutes. Do not describe the cap as
"fatigue", and do not claim that fatigue is absent.

The error that has actually been measured is the *shape* of the rotation: the model gives
its top six far more minutes than any real club does. That is the candidate for ADR 0005.

## Seasons

**`SEASON` / `season`** — always the **opening year**. `season = 2025` means the 2025/26
season. Display strings are derived: `f"{SEASON}/{(SEASON + 1) % 100:02d}"`.

Never write a season as a hard-coded string. `salary_external_2025.csv` stores
`"2025-2026"` in a `season` column and an opening year in `yr`; the `yr` column is the
canonical one.

## Price curve

**`β₁`** — the coefficient on `pir_lag_shrunk` in the cost model. It controls how steeply
price rises with production, and it carries essentially all of the price dispersion.
**β₁ is the only coefficient that meaningfully changes which players the LP selects.**

Everything else in the cost model — the intercept, `smear`, the club effects under
`market`, and the pool-mean normalisation — multiplies every player's price by the same
factor. Scaling all costs and the budget by the same factor leaves the LP's feasible set
unchanged, so it cannot change the selection.
